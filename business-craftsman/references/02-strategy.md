# Strategic Formulation & Competitive Moats

> **Core Principle**: Strategy is not a collection of aspirational goals; strategy is the deliberate allocation of scarce resources to build defensible competitive advantage while explicitly choosing what NOT to do.

---

## 1. The Strategy Statement Taxonomy

Every statement in a strategic brief must be categorized explicitly:

| Category | Definition | Governance Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FACT** | Empirically verified current reality. | Must be backed by data (financial, operational, customer). |
| **ASSUMPTION** | An unproven belief accepted as true for now. | Must have a documented risk trigger if proven wrong. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A testable proposition about cause and effect. | Must have an experiment, success metric, and decision deadline. |
| **DECISION** | A chosen path between competing alternatives. | Recorded in a Decision Record with trade-offs and rejected options. |
| **COMMITMENT** | Irreversible capital or contract allocation. | Demands executive approval, verified business case, and exit plan. |

---

## 2. The 5 Defensible Competitive Moats

Initiatives should reinforce at least one structural moat:
1. **Network Effects**: Value increases for every user as new users join (e.g. marketplaces, communication networks).
2. **Switching Costs**: Prohibitive friction, data integration, or workflow inertia to migrate to a competitor.
3. **Cost Advantage**: Structurally lower cost of goods sold (COGS) through scale, proprietary technology, or operational efficiency.
4. **Intangible Assets / Brand**: Regulatory licenses, patents, or trusted brand authority.
5. **Counter-Positioning**: A business model that incumbents cannot copy without destroying their existing core business.
