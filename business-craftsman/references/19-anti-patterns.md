# Organizational Anti-Patterns & Corrective Actions

> **Core Principle**: Recognize organizational pathology early. Organizational theater destroys capital and demoralizes top talent.

---

## 1. Top 8 Organizational Anti-Patterns

### 1. The Feature Factory
- *Why it happens*: Pressure to show visible activity without measuring post-launch adoption.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Bloats codebase and operational complexity with zero measurable ROI.
- *Early Signal*: Backlog prioritized by stakeholder requests rather than verified customer problems.
- *Corrective Action*: Institute mandatory 60-day Value Realization Reviews and feature sunset quotas.

### 2. Sunk-Cost Attachment
- *Why it happens*: Emotional attachment and fear of admitting past misjudgment.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Throws good capital after bad while starving high-potential new initiatives.
- *Early Signal*: "We've already invested \$2M and 9 months, we can't stop now."
- *Corrective Action*: Apply the Re-Funding Test (*"Would we fund this from scratch today?"*).

### 3. Status Theater & Watermelon Reporting
- *Why it happens*: Cultural fear of transparently reporting blockers or delays to executives.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Conceals critical risks until the deadline passes, making recovery impossible.
- *Early Signal*: Projects reported "GREEN" until 48 hours before launch, suddenly flipping to RED.
- *Corrective Action*: Base project health strictly on automated verification evidence and dependency risks.

### 4. HiPPO Prioritization (Highest Paid Person's Opinion)
- *Why it happens*: Deferring to executive hierarchy rather than customer evidence.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Builds products based on internal executive assumptions that fail in the market.
- *Early Signal*: "The CEO had an idea over the weekend, drop everything."
- *Corrective Action*: Require all strategic ideas to pass Gate 1 Problem Discovery and Experiment Briefs.

### 5. Meeting-as-Work Culture
- *Why it happens*: Diffusing personal accountability through large alignment meetings.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Fragments maker time, inflates lead times, and substitutes discussion for execution.
- *Early Signal*: Calendars booked 8 hours/day with zero clear decision records produced.
- *Corrective Action*: Default to asynchronous written proposals; meetings require an explicit Decision Owner.

### 6. Velocity Theater (Story Point Inflation)
- *Why it happens*: Weaponizing story points as an individual productivity metric.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Teams artificially inflate estimates while real customer lead time degrades.
- *Early Signal*: Team velocity doubles, but time-to-market and customer value stay flat.
- *Corrective Action*: Abandon velocity as a performance target; measure customer Lead Time and Flow Throughput.

### 7. Hero Culture & Unsustainable Crunch
- *Why it happens*: Chronic under-estimation and refusal to make scope trade-offs.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Causes severe employee burnout, high attrition, and introduces critical production bugs.
- *Early Signal*: Successful launches depend on specific individuals working 80-hour weeks.
- *Corrective Action*: Enforce the 80% Capacity Utilization Rule and realistic probabilistic forecasting ($P_{80}$).

### 8. Premature Scaling & Premature Hiring
- *Why it happens*: Mistaking early validation signals for broad product-market fit.
- *Why it is dangerous*: Spikes monthly burn rate before unit economics and retention are proven.
- *Early Signal*: Doubling engineering headcount while customer churn remains high.
- *Corrective Action*: Freeze hiring until Customer LTV/CAC > 3x and Net Revenue Retention > 100%.
