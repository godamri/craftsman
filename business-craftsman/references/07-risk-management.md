# Enterprise Risk Management & Threat Governance

> **Core Principle**: Risk management is not a static quarterly compliance spreadsheet; it is an active operational discipline that prevents catastrophic surprises.

---

## 1. The 4 Enterprise Risk Tiers

| Tier | Severity | Definition | Mandatory Governance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P0: EXISTENTIAL** | Fatal | Threatens company solvency, regulatory license, or complete business viability. | C-Suite / Board visibility, daily tracking, active contingency plan, instant stop-loss trigger. |
| **P1: MAJOR** | Severe | Causes material financial loss (>\$500k), significant customer churn, or major delivery delay (>1 month). | Executive Sponsor ownership, weekly risk review, dedicated mitigation budget. |
| **P2: MATERIAL** | Moderate | Causes localized delivery delays (1–4 weeks), budget overrun, or minor operational impairment. | Product & Engineering Lead ownership, standard sprint mitigation tracking. |
| **P3: MINOR** | Low | Routine operational friction or minor bug with straightforward workarounds. | Tracked in backlog; resolved during normal delivery cadence. |

---

## 2. Risk Register Anatomy

Every identified risk must record:
- **Risk Title & Root Cause**: Clear description of what might happen and why.
- **Probability (1–5) & Impact (1–5)**: Calculated Risk Exposure score ($P \times I$).
- **Early Warning Trigger Indicator**: Leading metric that alerts the team before impact occurs.
- **Preventive Mitigation**: Actions taken now to reduce probability.
- **Contingency Plan**: Action taken if the risk event actually materializes.
- **Named Owner**: The single individual responsible for tracking and executing the mitigation.
